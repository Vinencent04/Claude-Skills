# Style notes from the reference clip (study only: no frames, story or characters are reused)
Clip: 45 s, 1080x1920, 30 fps, ~14 shots, one word per caption chunk (~0.4 s each).

## Camera grammar (what actually happens)
- Opens at street level in a night alley, characters small in a lit doorway; camera glides forward/down, then
  tilts to a steep top-down aerial over the neon street (descent from height), hard cut to the next place.
- Long corridor dolly: one-point perspective, rows of props receding to a vanishing point, characters in the
  foreground lean into the lens at the edge of the frame; the camera keeps moving forward the whole time.
- Follow shot behind a character carrying the KEY PROP: the prop grows until it fills the frame while its word is said.
- Doorway framing: warm interior light vs cold blue exterior, wet reflective ground; character walks past a door.
- Counter scenes: medium shots, two characters, camera pushes in slowly; each new word/idea = a small prop pop or face change.
- Alley with shallow depth of field: heavy blur on foreground/back, small sharp subject in the glow of a window, creeping push-in.
- Ending: tilt/pull to a readable in-world sign (the contrast line) with all characters in frame.
- Shot length 1.5-5 s, one idea per shot, a cut at nearly every sentence, place changes on cuts, never long static holds.

## Look
- Complementary light: amber/orange warm interiors against cold blue-violet night, magenta/cyan neon accents.
- Characters: cream bean head, dark outline, very long thin stick legs, tiny body, saturated one-colour torso
  (orange, purple, blue), simple faces with big expression changes (grin, deadpan, side-eye).
- World: 3D set with ink lines, painted textures, readable in-world signs (big text on props), a cat as a background gag.
- Captions: white, bold, black outline, centered at ~70-80 % of the height (above the bottom edge), 1-3 words, never over the face/key prop.
