# The War of Jenkins' Ear (1731-1748), 87 s, voice only (ElevenLabs James, eleven_v3 with emotion tags, stability 0 / style 1 / speed 1.2), camera = holds + quick eased travels, smoother (slower, finer-quantised) animation
Documented: 1731 Captain Robert Jenkins' brig is boarded by a Spanish coast guard off Cuba/Florida; his ear is cut off (he said so later); Jenkins kept it and, in 1738, is said to have shown it to Parliament; Britain declared war on Spain in October 1739 (Walpole reluctant, "now they ring the bells, soon they will wring their hands"); Vernon took Portobelo 1739; Cartagena de Indias, March-May 1741: Blas de Lezo (one eye, one arm, one leg) with ~6 ships and ~3000 men defeated the British fleet of ~180 ships and ~25000 men, mostly lost to disease; the conflict merged into the War of the Austrian Succession (ends 1748); name coined by Thomas Carlyle in 1858.
Flag / unverified: the quote "Go tell your king, he's next" (paraphrase of what Jenkins reported); that Jenkins held the ear up in Parliament (traditional, disputed); "thousands died, mostly disease" (standard estimate); the officer's exact words and the crowd scenes are staged colour.
CTA ties back to the story: "if you like history, where a single ear shakes up the world, follow for more stories like this".
