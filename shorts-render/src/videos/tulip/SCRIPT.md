# Tulip mania (1637): the story vs. the myth, 85.1 s, voice only (ElevenLabs James, eleven_v3), accuracy-first
Documented / mainstream scholarship: tulips came from the Ottoman Empire to the Dutch Republic in the 1590s; "broken" (flame-striped) tulips were caused by a virus (identified in the 20th century); Semper Augustus is the famous variety, reported at thousands of guilders per bulb; futures-style contracts for bulbs still in the ground were signed in taverns; prices rose steeply in winter 1636/37; the market collapsed in early Feb 1637 (a failed auction in Haarlem); the bankrupt-families picture is largely a later myth (Anne Goldgar and others found little evidence); the "sailor ate a bulb" tale is a single anecdote of unclear truth; buyers' contracts were settled/cancelled for a small fraction (about 3.5%); the dramatic version was popularised by Charles Mackay's 1841 book.
Flagged: "more than a fancy house" (price comparisons depend on the source); "almost no proof" simplifies a scholarly debate; the 3.5% figure refers to the later settlement.
